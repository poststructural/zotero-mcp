# Strand framing arguments (accepted by Austin)

Each strand's introduction states its framing argument early, the section openers and the "Summary of Key Findings" return to it, and its claims stay within what the reviewed studies show. These are Austin's accepted arguments; judge drafts against them rather than inferring a thesis from the draft. Where a draft's wording overreaches the evidence for its argument, flag it; do not change the argument itself.

## Canon and Canon Wars (accepted Nov 18, 2026)
The secondary canon shows continuity in the face of the canon wars. Change came mainly through additions, supplements and electives; required core works changed only where institutions supported replacement. (Dorothea's reading, confirmed by Austin.) The historical frame spans the formation of the American secondary canon and the university canon wars of 1982–1994; the review covers professional commentaries, survey studies of assigned reading, and qualitative studies of teachers' text selection.

## Censorship: "continuity, now codified" (accepted Nov 18, 2026)
Across both waves of controversy, local challenges of 1974–1999 and state policy since 2021, restriction has reached secondary English teachers' text selection mainly through ordinary selection and review procedures and teachers' anticipation of objections, more than through outright bans. The 2020s statutes codify procedures that earlier local disputes built and extend their reach. This strand defines the shared terms challenge, ban, rejection and restriction. Austin added a short theoretical opening on why the taxonomy is needed (Nov 22); it is his own passage.

## Moral Panics: "the alarm passes, the restriction persists" (accepted Nov 18, 2026)
Panics cast teachers, and later their texts, as threats to children. The alarm subsides, but employment risk, materials filtered before they reach teachers, and anticipated objection persist and shape selection. Structure: moral panic theory (Cohen; Goode & Ben-Yehuda); historical episodes ordered by start year; contemporary campaigns since 2017; survey and qualitative studies; summary.

## Text Selection: "reported autonomy, inherited canon" (confirmed Nov 18, 2026)
Since the canon wars, teachers report wide autonomy but exercise it within departmental, material and political conditions that favor works already in place; selection itself reproduces canonical continuity. The strand keeps a section on text sets as a curricular intervention (Nov 20) and ends, after its Summary of Key Findings, with Austin's own theoretical move: "defensibility" and the "canon-practice apparatus." That closing section is theory by design (GROUND, then SYNTHESIZE, then THEORIZE); review it for clarity and attribution, not for absence of empirical detail.
