# Study-paragraph templates and workflow lessons from the Canon revision (Nov 18, 2026)

Source: Dorothea's 11.16 comments on Canon v5 and her email. The Canon v6 revision applies these: `/mnt/project-files/Canon_CanonWars/Canon_CanonWars.v6_DA-11.16-revisions.docx`. Austin has not accepted it yet. Use these as patterns. Never fill a slot from memory. If the source audit doesn't have a slot, mark it [CHECK].

## 1. Qualitative study paragraph (Dorothea's model, C228)
Order:
1. **Genre + site + purpose.** "In a [case study / interview study / PAR study / thesis] of [site], [Author] (year) examined [purpose]."
2. **Data and participants.** What was observed (observations *of what*), who was interviewed, how long, what documents.
3. **What the study traces.** Name the agents. People replace books; books don't replace books (C150).
4. **Specific finding.** Concrete particulars: the title, the objection, the decision.
5. **What it documents about text selection.** One or two sentences, phrased as the study's contribution ("The study documents teachers' role in preserving the canon even amid deliberate efforts to expand it").
- Do **not** add a generalizability caveat to each study (email point 4). If one is needed, put a single sentence in the subsection opener saying what these studies offer that surveys can't.
- Don't attribute a purpose the study didn't have (C232).

## 2. Survey study paragraph (email point 1, C197)
Order:
1. **Sponsor/genre + year of data + who responded** (schools, department chairs, teachers, former students).
2. **What respondents were asked to report.** Spell out the unit: works required of all students in a class, versus works a teacher assigned in a course, versus selections from the last five days, versus graduates' recollections. Say what counted and what didn't, in plain words (C168).
3. **Sample and response.**
4. **Findings**, with each figure tied to its measure and grade band.
5. **Author's interpretation**, attributed and kept separate from the findings.
- When one author reports several studies (Applebee), first list the studies and the publication reporting each, then take them in turn. Label every paragraph with which survey it is about (C186-190).
- When a source slices data several ways (grade 9-12 versus 7-12, track, school population), announce the slices before giving the numbers (email point 2, C171).
- Report proportions in words a reader can hold: "Only three titles were assigned in as many as one in five courses" (C196).

## 3. Headings (email point 6, C222)
- Top-level headings are parallel and form the argument's road map. Drop labels like "Interlude:".
- Review subsections are named by **genre + date span**, in parallel: "Professional Commentaries on X, 1985-2001", "Survey Studies of X, 1963-2023", "Qualitative Studies of X, 1991-2021".
- The review section's opener names the genres in the order they come, and states the key finding (C131, C135).
- Each subsection opens with what its sources show, not with a study.

## 4. Tie studies to the strand's frame (email point 5)
- The qualitative-subsection opener places each study relative to the strand's historical frame (during, local form, aftermath) and says why it was selected.
- The strand thesis (for Canon: continuity in the face of the canon wars) appears in the intro, the section opener, the qualitative opener, and the summary.

## 5. Political stances (C72, C108, C110)
- Identify actors' positions (conservative, progressive, opponent of X). "You're disguising the political stances of the actors."
- When several scholars stand for different camps, separate them, label each camp, and say explicitly that they conflict (C60, C68).

## 6. Summary (C233-234)
- Revise it last. It restates each section's main points in order. Introduce nothing new: Dorothea's "I didn't realize that" flags a finding the body never foregrounded.
